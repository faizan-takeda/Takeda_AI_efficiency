Story 01 · Portfolio truth

The lighthouse without a harbour map

A strong AI signal can make an organisation look advanced while leadership still cannot see duplicate routes, missing owners, total run cost or which products actually reached sustained use.

Portfolio truth = owner + route + stage + next decision
Real-life story

A bright signal is not a portfolio map.

A coastal kingdom had an excellent lighthouse. Every captain could report movement, yet the harbour master could not see that two ships carried the same cargo, one voyage had no accountable owner and several never reached port.

The technology worked. The operating system did not.

Takeda relevance

Takeda has public evidence of AI investment, internal GenAI products, ICCs and a Transformation Office. The executive gap is a certified, comparable view across all of them.

Solution

One ID, one owner, one stage, total cost, evidence status and next decision date for every AI, analytics, automation and agent product.

Leadership proof

Register completeness, duplicate routes merged, stage ageing and explicit stop decisions.

Use Takeda’s own strongest analogy

Takeda would not advance a molecule because a team demonstrated scientific activity. It advances on defined evidence, named accountability and an explicit portfolio decision. AI assets need the same discipline, adapted to digital products.

Internal transfer · stage discipline

Do not create a central delivery bottleneck

The portfolio mechanism should certify truth and force decisions. Product teams remain local and fast; the shared layer only standardises identity, evidence, reuse and decision rights.

Design principle · common truth, local delivery

Story 02 · Product, not project

The exhibition machine and the working railway

A prototype proves motion. A product proves reliable service on an ordinary Monday — with users, monitoring, ownership, maintenance, economics and a path to retirement.

Prototype proves motion · product proves service
Real-life story

The launch date is not the finish line.

An engineering guild built a beautiful train for a world exhibition. It moved perfectly for one hundred metres. The city later discovered that operating a railway required timetables, maintenance, safety checks, trained staff, monitoring and daily passengers.

The prototype answered “can it move?” The product answered “can it serve?”

Problem

Short project cycles reward demos and hand-offs. They rarely fund adoption, run ownership, improvement and retirement.

Product shift

Fund an enduring user outcome, not a temporary system. The owner remains accountable from problem definition through run and retirement.

Proof

Eligible-user adoption, successful-workflow rate, cost per successful outcome, benefit persistence and migration completion.

Move from building systems to building products.

Facebook, Google Maps, Instagram and AlphaFold did not become important because they were “completed” in a 90-day window. They became products that kept improving through repeated releases, user evidence, platform investment and clear ownership.

A pilot may be time-boxed. The product outcome is not. Each successful Takeda AI capability needs a roadmap, a run owner, an adoption loop and an explicit retirement path.

Story 03 · Shared foundations

The city that rebuilt the same bridge

Five districts can each deliver a bridge and still leave the city with five designs, five rulebooks, five repair teams and five future migrations. Local delivery looks fast; enterprise change becomes expensive.

Shared foundations · local products · funded migration
Real-life story

Reuse is not a catalogue. It is a funded migration path.

Each district built quickly because it controlled its own bridge. The city paid later: incompatible designs, repeated safety reviews, fragmented repair contracts and no common way to upgrade.

Shared foundations are valuable only when they are easier to adopt than local reinvention.

Problem

Repeated data discovery, connectors, model plumbing, evaluation and control evidence consume engineering capacity before the user problem is addressed.

Solution

Product catalogue, data contracts, stable APIs, reusable controls, funded migration and planned deprecation.

Proof

Reuse rate, time-to-usable-data, duplicate components retired, exception ageing and cost avoided.

NVIDIA · platform compounding

Build the substrate that improves every product.

NVIDIA describes a full-stack accelerated computing platform spanning CUDA, domain libraries, frameworks, SDKs and APIs. The transferable lesson is compounding: stable foundations make each new use case faster to build and easier to operate.

  • Fund shared capabilities as products with roadmaps and service levels.
  • Keep local teams free to build differentiated workflows above the platform.
  • Measure adoption and retirement, not catalogue size.
  • Require a substitution path so model and vendor changes do not force a rebuild.

Story 04 · Earned autonomy

Autonomy is a release decision, not a label

The important question is not whether a workflow is “agentic.” It is what the system may do, how reversible the action is, what evidence cleared the release and who can stop it.

Authority rises only when evidence and safeguards rise
Real-life story

The autopilot earns authority one reversible step at a time.

An aircraft does not move from manual control to full autonomy because the software is impressive. It advances through defined operating boundaries, evidence, human checkpoints, monitoring and rehearsed fallback.

Greater authority requires stronger proof and stronger safeguards.

Problem

“Agentic” is often used as a technology label while action scope, reversibility and release evidence remain unclear.

Solution

Tier every product by authority and impact. Promote only when task evals, control evidence, permissions, human override and rollback are complete.

Proof

Pre-release eval pass rate, override frequency, incident severity, rollback readiness and post-release drift.

0

Assist

Summarise, retrieve, draft or classify. A person performs the consequential action.

Evidence: task quality, traceability, privacy and user verification.
1

Recommend

Propose a decision or action. A named person approves before execution.

Evidence: decision quality, calibrated confidence and review design.
2

Act within limits

Execute reversible actions using approved tools, permissions and hard constraints.

Evidence: tool tests, permission boundaries, audit and rollback rehearsal.
3

High impact

Take actions with material patient, regulatory, financial or operational consequence.

Evidence: formal safety case, executive risk acceptance and fail-safe operation.

The decision

Prove the mechanism on three live products.

Do not start with a new AI programme. Select real products already carrying users, cost and organisational complexity. Instrument them through one complete portfolio cycle and force an explicit decision.
