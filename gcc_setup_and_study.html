Four challenges.
One root cause.

Evidence from Google and Amazon's own operating models, Bain-grade transformation economics, and seventy years of pharmaceutical R&D data. Not four separate problems, but one problem wearing four different sets of clothes.

Nobody owns anything end to end. The Satellite trap is missing ownership of a product. Dependency drag is missing ownership of an interface. The data estate is missing ownership of a foundation.

Credit where it is due. Bain finds the successful 12% "often appoint a dedicated Chief Transformation Officer." Takeda has done that. This brief concerns the four boxes not yet ticked.

Exhibit A · The base raten = 100

88 of every 100 business transformations fail to achieve their original ambition, measured against stated ambition in full. Bain estimates more than a third of large organisations are transforming at any given time.

The seventy-year warning

Pharma has already run the experiment we are about to run

Every technology wave was supposed to fix R&D productivity. Genomics arrived. High-throughput screening arrived. Combinatorial chemistry, computer-aided design and x-ray crystallography all became orders of magnitude cheaper. The curve below did not bend once.

Exhibit B · Eroom's Law, 1950–2010Log scale · new drugs per $1B R&D
Observed trend Counterfactual, never tested

New drugs approved per billion dollars of inflation-adjusted R&D spend halved roughly every nine years, a ~100-fold decline between 1950 and 2010. Named by Scannell et al. (2012): Moore spelled backwards. Cost per approved drug has compounded at 8–9% a year in real terms for seven decades. The dashed line is illustrative of a counterfactual, not a claim.

AI will not be the exception unless the operating model changes with it.

The synthesis

Fix ownership and you fix all four

All four are the same challenge wearing different clothes: nobody owns anything end to end. This is why Amazon's single-threaded leadership is the highest-leverage import on the list, and why Challenge 02 is the one to read twice.
